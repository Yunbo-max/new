# R003/B1 — Probe angle is not a basis-invariant causal mechanism

**Scientific question:** The 2026 knowing–doing gap reports late-layer near-orthogonal decodable directions for necessity and action. Is their cosine angle sufficient evidence that necessity is not used by the action policy?

**Information boundary/operator:** At a single pre-action history h, suppose the hidden representation is a 2-vector H; latent necessity N and tool-action logit A are separately linear readouts, s_N=w_N^T H and s_A=w_A^T H. What matters for causal action control is a controlled intervention on H and its effect on action probability and hidden task correctness, not just ordinary probe accuracy.

**Formal counterexample (exact; geometry only):** Initially w_N=(1,0), w_A=(0,1), angle cosine 0. Let H'=M H where M=[[1,100],[0,1]] is invertible. Replace both readouts by w'=M^{-T} w. Then s'_j=w_j'^T H'=w_j^T H for every H: the observed cognitive and action scores are *identical*, but transformed probe vectors are w'_N=(1,-100), w'_A=(0,1), with cosine -100/sqrt(10001)≈-0.999950. Thus probe cosine changes from exactly 0 to near -1 without any observable change in the two readout functions. The code checked 4 binary hidden inputs and found maximum score difference 0.

**Scope caveat:** This is a change-of-coordinates equivalence in a readout model, NOT proof that a trained Transformer with RMSNorm etc admits arbitrary GL(2) symmetry or that the same model's fixed learned basis makes angles meaningless. The lesson is simply that angles are not *by themselves* functional evidence of disconnection. Probe directions are covectors; transported interventions and decoder Jacobians matter.

**Rivals/simple repairs:** The original paper's orthogonality may still be useful as a within-model diagnostic; actual hidden-state steering can change call rates. Compare pure cost-aware decision threshold, Probe&Prefill and learned action-direction intervention with matched norms/hidden correctness.
