# R003/B4 — The exact boundary where one-step greedy effort allocation is already optimal

**Scientific question:** When is it wrong to call an agent's myopic computation strategy inefficient?

**Information boundary/operator:** N independent tasks, integer effort t_i>=0, known payoff p_i(t_i), sum_i t_i=B. This assumes no information coupling, adaptive observations or stochastic stopping; actual LLM problems can violate all of these assumptions.

**Proposition (standard result, NOT a novel theorem):** If every discrete increment d_i(k)=p_i(k)-p_i(k-1) is nonnegative and nonincreasing in k, repeatedly assign the next effort unit to the largest available marginal gain. This greedy allocation is optimal. Proof sketch: each task requires selecting a prefix of a descending marginal sequence; if greedy's chosen unit were not among the largest feasible gains then exchanging an allocated smaller marginal for a feasible larger marginal improves the objective. Descending property ensures any desired higher marginal in a task is preceded by at least as high a marginal, so a greedy procedure can acquire it. Under the stated separable uniform-cost assumptions, classical discrete concave resource allocation settles it.

**Counterexample when concavity fails:** easy p=(.80,.82,.84,.86) and hard p=(.15,.15,.15,.80), B=3. Greedy gives all 3 steps to easy: 1.01 expected correct. Exact enumeration gives all 3 to hard: 1.60. Gap .59, driven by delayed complementarity. Synthetic, not LLM evidence.

**Executed check:** Enumerated 35 nonincreasing-marginal curves (increments in {0,.02,.04,.06}) for each of two tasks with B=4. Across 1225 curve pairs, greedy matched exact enumeration with 0 violations. This is a finite exhaustive check of that generated family, not a general proof.

**Nearest prior-art:** Federgruen & Groenevelt 1986 https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.34.6.909 already formalized concave resource allocation and greedy conditions. 2026 OptimalThinkingBench https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/0f63515b14f33c008158213c7b6191c6-Abstract-Conference.html and 2026 adaptive compute studies cover over/underthinking. MUTO and CATS were screened in R002. A general 'greedy can fail on a nonconcave payoff' claim has almost no novelty.

**Rivals/repair:** Standard dynamic programming or finite-horizon lookahead on estimated p_i; train a small value-of-computation predictor with careful held-out data and compare to strong cost-adaptive baselines. A model can appear to overthink because its output probability is nonmonotone or generation differs; do not treat token count alone as effort.

**Falsifier/protocol:** Empirically estimate per-task p_i(t) for multiple budgets and seeds, check confidence intervals for nonconcavity and delayed reward, evaluate a *learned* controller vs greedy, DP-with-oracle only as upper bound, CTA/MUTO/CATS at equal total cost. If observed per-task curves are adequately concave under uncertainty or established adaptive methods solve the nonconcave cases, no new mechanism remains.
