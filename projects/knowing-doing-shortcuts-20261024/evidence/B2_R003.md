# R003/B2 — In stochastic decisions, observed cost response still may not identify model motives

**Scientific question:** Does varying a tool's price and observing the choice probability identify subjective benefit v and effort aversion lambda?

**Information boundary/operator:** Observe A in {0,1} and randomized displayed price c after a matched pre-decision history h; private beliefs and inverse-temperature beta are not observed. A stylized model samples A~Bernoulli(sigmoid(beta*(v-lambda*c))). The ratio/scales are analyst hypotheses, not proven internal arithmetic of an LLM.

**Formal nonidentifiability (exact):** For every alpha>0, setting (v',lambda',beta')=(alpha*v,alpha*lambda,beta/alpha) leaves beta'(v'-lambda'*c)=beta*(v-lambda*c) for ALL c; hence the full distribution of observed actions conditional on any price is identical. Without a noise-scale or anchor assumption, neither the subjective benefit nor cost coefficient has a unique numerical interpretation. Unlike the deterministic threshold-only proof in R002, this applies to logistic random choice but crucially relies on beta being unknown/free. If beta were calibrated/known and the logistic link genuinely correct, slope/intercept can identify the parameters.

**Executed numerical witness:** (v=.12,lambda=1,beta=3) and (v=.36,lambda=3,beta=1) yield choice-probability differences <=1.11e-16 across 101 prices. An independently specified forced-verification uplift of .20 and four deterministic-price choices produce toy mean preventable regret .0225. This is NOT evidence that an LLM has either belief system.

**Important distinction:** A positive action regret shows behavior violates the benchmark's *externally specified* reward objective, under conditional causal identification of forced-action potential outcomes. It does NOT identify internal motive, consciousness, or subjective price representations. Forced tool call must be an intervention after the same saved pre-action history, and subsequent policy/tool output/harness must be matched; simply placing 'always use tools' earlier in the prompt changes the treatment.

**Closest baselines/novelty collision:** Ding et al. Calibrate-Then-Act https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16699 and its coding-test harness https://github.com/NSF-Simons-CosmicAI-Institute/CalibrateThenAct ; Wu et al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00737 already distinguish perceived versus true need/utility and affordability. More prompt instructions or a controller are not a defensible novel result.

**Competitors/simple remedy:** A tool-value estimator trained on forced-action uplift, budget-aware CTA controller, explicit reliability feedback or inferred thresholds under calibrated beta. A proposed algorithm must outperform these at matched task information and tools.

**Falsifier/protocol:** 2x2 or multi-price within-task randomization with task-and-seed clustered uncertainty, frozen pre-action checkpoint and forced continuation for A=1/0. Estimate conditional regret with confidence bounds, compare to calibrated CTA and Wu utility estimators. If dominated skips are absent after matched baselines, no new failure mechanism is supported. If cost choices are explained by a single calibrated utility estimate, subjective 'effort suppression' is not independently identifiable.
