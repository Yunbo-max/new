## QKV-A: A local reduction of error can increase final prediction risk

**D1 — Actual information / objective.** A model has head errors `e_h` in the coordinates in which its readout is applied and proposed head updates `d_h`. Its *task-relevant* output error is `e=sum_h W_h e_h`, and aggregate update `d=sum_h W_h d_h`. The downstream squared-loss difference is

`Delta_R = E[||e+d||^2-||e||^2] = 2 E[e^T d] + E||d||^2`.

Per-head safety statements concern only terms such as `2E[e_h^T d_h]+E||d_h||^2`; these omit cross-head and projection cross terms. Even if their confidence intervals all cover and their individual risks do not increase, there is no guarantee that the **aggregated** loss improves.

**D2–D3 — Constructive proof and simple countercontrol.** Let `z~N(0,1)`, two readout blocks the identity, `e1=z`, `e2=-z`, so the original aggregate error vanishes *identically*. A calibration/update makes `e1'=0` (strictly improving head 1) while leaving `e2'=-z` (not harming head 2). The new aggregate error is `-z`, MSE 1. Thus the two heads are individually non-worse and one is strictly improved, yet downstream accuracy decreases. The independent 100,000-draw CPU check found head1 MSE `.99244665 -> 0`, head2 `.99244665 -> .99244665`, fused `0 -> .99244665`.

A separate confidence problem compounds the gap: if 16 *independent* individual intervals each cover at 95%, joint coverage is `.95^16=.44012667`; across a hypothetical 28×16=448 independent head intervals it is `1.05e-10`. Independence is illustrative, NOT an assumption for actual transformer heads; Bonferroni provides familywise control given per-event rates but can make simultaneous safe action sparse. Even simultaneous per-head coverage would not repair the aggregate cancellation counterexample.

**D4 — Closest work/equivalence.** Ordinary output-level GLS, supervised task/readout calibration and full-covariance robust regression optimize the relevant quantity. *WitCert: Sound Runtime Risk Observability and Gating for KV-Cache Quantization* (arXiv:2607.28699v1) explicitly reports cross-layer error cancellation in its actual Qwen2.5 experiments and is a DIRECT empirical collision for simplistic local-error reasoning. The algebra is ordinary covariance accounting, NOT a novel QKV method.

**D5–D6 — Discriminating task protocol and disposition.** In a pinned model, measure changes at (i) attention logits and output of each head, (ii) after each `W_O` block, (iii) after the whole residual stream, and (iv) next-token KL/NLL and task correctness. Compare naive per-head safety, joint projected GLS/ridge, no intervention, output-aware calibration at identical labels, rank and FLOPs. Strong falsifier: ordinary joint readout and confidence calibration dominate without an architectural change. **Status:** `deep_incomplete`; corrects proxy objective, no native task or accepted design. **Next actor:** supervisor freezes task/readout interface, independent Work reviewer audits.

## QKV-A-signal-noise-identifiability: known label-flip prior does not transport to a new domain

**D1–D2.** Prior R012 gave correct Bayes mixture correction when a domain-wide label flip `F` has *known* probability `rho0`. With unlimited labels, the observable effective sign is `b=s(1-2F)`. For a symmetric prior `s=+/-1`, the fitted posterior mean correction coefficient is `a b`, `a=1-2rho0`. If deployment actually has `rho`, its asymptotic excess target MSE, in R012's scale with sign amplitude `g`, is

`R(rho0,rho) = 2g^2 [1+a^2-2a(1-2rho)]`.

No-label midpoint excess MSE is `2g^2`. For `a>0`, using the calibrated sign becomes worse than ignoring labels exactly when `rho > 1/2-a/4`.

**D3 — Exact numbers.** With `g=.25`, nominal `rho0=.3` (hence `a=.4`) the harm threshold is `rho>.4`. At true `rho=.3` excess is `.105`; at `.45` it is `.135`; at `.60` it is `.165`; no-label `.125`. These are exact limiting population risks, not sampled task-level runs. Under an adversarial unknown `rho ∈ [.2,.6]`, a robust signed-amplitude minimax strategy selects `a=0`, since both effective-sign orientations remain plausible. Granting accurate trusted anchors or shift metadata changes the information interface and may permit positive action.

**D4–D6 — Falsifier / route.** This is familiar robust decision theory. A learnable Bayesian calibration only supports QKV research if task supervision or trusted anchors exist within budget, and its native gains exceed ordinary ridge, mixture Bayes and an explicit abstention baseline under domain shift. The core result is a *negative* information/access boundary, not a new mechanism. **Status:** `deep_incomplete`; external label flips or real task interface unverified. **Next actor:** supervisor, independent reviewer before model work.
