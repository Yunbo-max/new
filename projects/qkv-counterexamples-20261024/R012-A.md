## A parent — Asymmetric confidence sets turn an abstract covariance assumption into a label-charged control

**Operational information.** Let centered observables `y1,D` have `a=Cov(y1,D)` and `v=Var(D)>0`. A task target `theta` is observed only in a *separate labeled calibration sample*. The candidate is `h_beta=y1+beta D`; unknown `c=Cov(theta,D)`. The original unlabeled data cannot identify `c` under arbitrary task definitions (R008, R011). Suppose a *valid externally derived* confidence interval `[c_l,c_u]` is available, e.g. from labeled samples. No target labels are visible to the deployed predictor after fitting.

**Exact minimax operator.** Task-risk difference is `R_c(beta)-R_c(0)=v beta^2+2 beta(a-c)`. With `c` in `[c_l,c_u]`, maximize over the interval endpoints and minimize over beta. Writing `c0=clip(a,c_l,c_u)` gives

`beta_rob = -(a-c0)/v`,  `max_{c in [c_l,c_u]} [R_c(beta_rob)-R_c(0)] = -dist(a,[c_l,c_u])^2 / v`.

Thus a robust correction is guaranteed non-worsening **only when the true hidden `c` is inside the externally justified interval**; an interval containing `a` forces beta=0. This is ordinary one-dimensional robust convex optimization, not a new attention layer.

**New CPU check.** Synthetic Gaussian `D~N(0,2)`, `theta=(c/2)D+epsilon`, epsilon~N(0,0.6^2), and *oracle-known population* `(a,v)=(0.75,2)`. A no-intercept OLS slope estimated from n labeled calibration pairs defines a Student-t 95% confidence interval, multiplied by the known v. The experiment used 3,000 independent calibration episodes for each `(c,n)`, n=8,32,128 and c∈{-0.2,0.2,0.8,1.1}. Observed interval coverage was approximately 0.948–0.956. On **every** sample whose interval covered the true c, the population risk change was <=0 (floating tolerance); this is guaranteed by the derivation, not independently validated task-level generalization. For c=0.2, the probability of applying a nonzero correction increased from .343 (n=8) to .927 (n=32) to 1.000 (n=128), while mean population risk change moved -0.0290, -0.0945, -0.1375. For c=.8 near observable a=.75, action rates were only .047, .067, .102, showing abstention when c≈a.

**Strongest simple alternatives.** Supervised ridge/GLS and calibrated confidence bounds can implement the same correction; CPU analysis assumes oracle-known `(a,v)` and exact Gaussian conditional model. A real experiment must charge *all* target labels, error bars, training/validation splits, estimated `(a,v)`, multiple-head multiplicity, and possible shift. There is no QKV architectural novelty in the clip function.

Otherwise demonstrate a fixed target representation, labeled calibration budget, and downstream NLL/accuracy improvement above ordinary ridge/multihead at equal information and FLOPs. ## A child — The correct likelihood mixture repairs a previous “many labels hurt” counterexample

**Information.** In R011, each domain has a latent true sign s∈{−1,+1} and an unobserved domain-wide flip F~Bernoulli(rho) of its noisy labels: `r_i=s(1−2F)g d_i+noise_i`, `d_i~N(0,2)`, noise variance `sigma2`. The target action still depends on s. R011's *misspecified* Bayes estimator assumes F=0 and can become worse with more labels. If rho is a known, validated prior (a NEW privilege), the observed labels identify the *effective* sign `b=s(1−2F)`; conditional symmetry implies

`E[s | observed labels] = (1−2rho) tanh(g sum_i d_i r_i / sigma2)`.

The quadratic-risk action is `beta=−1/2 + g E[s|labels]`. As n→∞, its average excess MSE converges to `8g² rho(1−rho)`; the misspecified original Bayes estimator converges to `8g² rho`. For rho=.3 and g=.25 these are **.105** versus **.15**, respectively; no-label midpoint risk is .125. The mixture-aware estimator never has higher Bayes risk than discarding its data *under the correctly specified joint prior*, so the original harmful-label phenomenon is a misspecification result, not a universal label-information paradox.

**Executed CPU check.** Independent 200,000-episode simulation at each n=4,16,64,256 used the exact Gaussian sufficient statistics `sum d_i²~2 ChiSquare(n)` and conditional Gaussian label statistic (seed 20261024, sigma2=1.47368421). At n=16: nominal-misspecified excess .14052, mixture-aware .11233; at n=256: .14931 vs .10473 (Monte Carlo). No pretrained LLM, actual oracle labels, or model tuning was run.

**A further identifiability obstruction.** With one domain and no trusted sign anchors, unknown rho and hidden s are generally inseparable from its effective observed sign. The correction assumes known rho; learning it requires independently anchored domains, structural restrictions, or task rewards, all charged resources. Ordinary robust Bayes/likelihood calibration is the mandatory rival method.

**Falsifier/next actor.** The mixture repair should be compared against ordinary supervised regression, likelihood calibration, and no-label fallback under equal trusted-anchor budgets and domain shifts. If it is simply robust-Bayes parity or task labels are unavailable, no distinct QKV method is justified. Formal reviewer and native model interface remain pending.
