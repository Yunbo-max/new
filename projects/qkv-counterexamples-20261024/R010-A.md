## A parent: exact robust optimizer under an externally bounded latent covariance

Fixed observable scalar D and baseline prediction y1, fitted correction h=y1+beta D; hidden target theta, residual epsilon=y1-theta. Center all variables and denote v=Var(D)>0, observed a=Cov(y1,D), hidden c=Cov(theta,D). True test squared-error change relative to beta=0 is

\[ \Delta R_c(\beta)=v\beta^2+2\beta(a-c). \]

Given defensible external bounds |c|<=C, the worst-case excess is W(beta)=v beta²+2a beta+2C|beta|, minimized by the classical soft-threshold solution

\[ \beta_{\rm robust}=-\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(a)(|a|-C)_+}{v},\qquad \min W=-\frac{(|a|-C)_+^2}{v}. \]

For a=.75,C=.4,v=2, a proxy-trained naive beta=-.375 incurs worst-case excess +.01875, while robust beta=-.175 incurs worst-case excess -.06125; beta=0 gives zero. When C>=|a|, the only minimax choice is zero adjustment. This is not a novel mathematical method, and C cannot in general be recovered from unlabeled (y1,D); supervised/ridge/GLS baselines are mandatory.

**Actual evidence:** exact algebra, closed convex minimization and CPU grid/assertions in R010_cpu_probe.py. **Falsifier:** real QKV heads have no shared identifiable target, or the uncertainty bound C is not justified by available task information, or ordinary same-information methods resolve the effect. **Next:** define a task-valid target and held-out observable features; have a separate independent Work reviewer inspect this exact interface before proposing a new attention operator.

## A child: posterior shrinkage strictly outperforms the hard-sign extrapolation

Reuse the R009 Gaussian two-world interface, with hidden s in {+1,-1} equally likely, known g>0, d_i~N(0,2), labelled residual r_i=s g d_i+zeta_i, iid zeta_i~N(0,sigma_eff²). From Gaussian likelihood,

\[ \log\frac{p(r\mid s=1,d)}{p(r\mid s=-1,d)}=\frac{2g}{\sigma_{\rm eff}^2}\sum_i d_i r_i,\qquad \mu=\mathbb{E}[s\mid r,d]=\tanh\left(\frac{g}{\sigma_{\rm eff}^2}\sum_i d_ir_i\right). \]

The Bayes squared-loss coefficient is beta=-1/2+g mu. Its expected oracle excess risk is 2g² E[(mu-s)²] = 2g² E[1-mu²] <=2g², with strict improvement for informative labels. Hard sign β=-1/2+g sign(mu) instead incurs excess 8g² P_sign_error, potentially >2g² if few labels. Thus the R009 observation that n4 labels hurt holds for HARD sign only, not Bayes-optimal label use; more valid information never makes an ideal decision worse.

For T=9, g=.25 and Gaussian label noise variance1 (sigma_eff²=1.473684...), numerical Gaussian/Hermite-Gamma quadrature yielded:

| n labels | Hard sign excess MSE | Bayes posterior mean excess MSE | No-label midpoint |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 0.147874 | **0.094651** | 0.125000 |
| 16 | 0.065277 | **0.046528** | 0.125000 |
| 64 | 0.005744 | **0.004354** | 0.125000 |

No LLM task labels were created or used. If true prior, noise, or loss changes, the closed-form posterior may no longer be optimal. A supervised ridge/Bayesian head and a common target should be the chief controls.
